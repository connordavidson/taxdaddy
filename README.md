# Taxdaddy

Taxdaddy is a browser-only planner for U.S. federal retirement income, tax, and investment growth. **Tax year 2027 is the first supported year.** The rates and limits apply to 2027 even though the IRS published them in 2026.

## Run and test

Run `python3 -m http.server 8000 --directory dist` from the checkout and open [localhost](http://localhost:8000). Use a static web server for the JavaScript modules. There are no dependencies, backend requests, cookies, or saved financial inputs.

Run `node --test tax.test.mjs growth.test.mjs` with Node.js 18 or newer. The [tax tests](tax.test.mjs) cover bracket boundaries, dividend stacking, RMD timing and cohorts, Roth conversion basis, the temporary senior deduction, and withdrawal attribution. The [growth tests](growth.test.mjs) cover compounding, recurring withdrawals, conversion transfers, and account depletion.

## Account balances and withdrawals

On the Scenario tab, each owner pairs their traditional IRA, non-Roth 401(k), Roth IRA, and Roth 401(k) balance with an expected cash withdrawal. Cash savings and taxable brokerage have household-level pairs. Use the prior December 31 balance as the starting point. Expected withdrawals are gross cash for the selected tax year. IRA and non-Roth 401(k) totals include their RMD portions. The RMD controls and **Use minimums** assign part of the total to the RMD and increase the total only if it is too small. IRA-to-Roth conversions remain separate internal transfers.

Enter the full cash coming out of taxable brokerage. In the withdrawal details, identify any portion from dividends, interest, or gains already entered under Annual income. The rest is modeled as principal. For example, a $12,000 withdrawal with $3,000 of gains counts as $9,000 of principal and $3,000 of gains already reported in income. The growth chart removes $12,000. Enter wages from part-time work or other ordinary income under **Other taxable income**. The model includes that amount in ordinary income and cash.

## Federal tax business logic

The [projection](dist/tax.mjs#L81) selects the requested tax year's [published rules](dist/tax-rules/catalog.mjs#L11), calculates each owner's distributions, computes household AGI and deductions, and applies federal taxes. Calendar-year amounts are in U.S. dollars. The household includes the second owner only for a joint return. Each spouse retains their own IRA basis and RMD calculations.

### Reading the withdrawal bracket chart

[Withdrawal attribution](dist/tax.mjs#L113) takes the final scenario's ordinary income, taxable Social Security, and deductions. It removes the sources in the chart from that ordinary income and layers taxable amounts back in this order: Roth IRA earnings, Roth 401(k) earnings, traditional IRA cash, non-Roth 401(k) cash, then IRA conversions. The chart displays the dollars covered by each band. Tax-free cash, basis recovery, and amounts covered by remaining deductions share the green 0% band. Higher ordinary brackets progress to dark red. Allocating the same withdrawals in a different order can change the displayed split by source. The chart shows ordinary brackets only. NIIT, additional taxes, and preferential-tax effects still appear in the tax projection. [Chart rendering](dist/app.mjs#L391)

### Reading the investment growth chart

The [growth model](dist/growth.mjs#L28) compares the same starting balances with and without the expected withdrawals from the Scenario tab. The combined investment view includes taxable brokerage, traditional IRA, non-Roth 401(k), Roth IRA, and Roth 401(k). Cash savings has a separate 0% growth view. Brokerage growth uses the full withdrawal including any portion reported separately as investment income for tax purposes.

Both paths apply the same nominal return once per year. The model grows each balance, applies planned year-end cash withdrawals up to the funds available, and makes the same funded IRA-to-Roth transfer in both paths in the selected tax year. IRA cash comes out before the conversion and Roth cash after it. Choose a single year's withdrawals or repeat the amount in nominal dollars. The ending-balance gap includes withdrawn dollars. The chart separately reports the growth effect after subtracting those dollars. Under a negative return, withdrawing earlier can avoid modeled losses. [Account mapping](dist/growth.mjs#L5) · [Growth view](dist/growth-view.mjs#L3)

The editable **5% nominal** starting assumption is an illustration. Schwab's 2026 ten-year forecasts are 5.9% for U.S. large-cap stocks and 4.8% for U.S. aggregate bonds. A simple 40% stock and 60% bond blend implies roughly 5.2%; there is no single retirement-account return. Constant compounding leaves out the order and volatility of market returns, inflation, future taxes or RMD calculations, and contributions. The input can reflect expected investment fees. [Default assumption](dist/growth.mjs#L3) · [Schwab 2026 capital market expectations](https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/schwabs-long-term-capital-market-expectations) · [Vanguard on sequence-of-returns risk](https://www.vanguard.co.uk/content/dam/intl/europe/documents/en/whitepapers/safeguarding-retirement-bear-market.pdf)

## Add or update a tax year

1. Verify the applicable year's IRS inflation adjustments in the [Internal Revenue Bulletin](https://www.irs.gov/irb) and check annual retirement guidance in [IRS retirement plan cost-of-living adjustments](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/cola-increases-for-dollar-limitations-on-benefits-and-contributions). Check statutory changes, effective dates, final regulations, and that year's form instructions where available. An announcement's publication year can differ from the tax year it governs.
2. Add a complete snapshot beside [2027.mjs](dist/tax-rules/2027.mjs#L17). Keep earlier snapshots intact. Record the official release URLs and review ordinary bracket caps and rates, preferential bands, standard and age deductions, Social Security and NIIT rules, RMD ages and factors, and temporary rules such as the senior deduction. Flat rates and thresholds belong in the snapshot. Changes to the calculation itself belong in the relevant [engine function](dist/tax.mjs) with explicit year or policy selection.
3. Register the new snapshot in the [year catalog](dist/tax-rules/catalog.mjs) and change its default when ready. The browser offers a year selector when more than one profile is registered. Dates, the bracket table, and annual source references are derived from the selected profile. Unsupported years fail with a clear error. The app never substitutes another year's rules.
4. Keep existing-year tests and add sourced expected results for the new year. Check exact bracket boundaries, filing statuses, phaseouts and sunsets, RMD cohort transitions, and the cutoff between qualified dividends and ordinary income. Verify the Scenario, Growth, and Tax rules views for each supported year.

## Scope and known gaps

The estimate excludes credits, withholding, state tax, AMT, payroll taxes, tax-table rounding, capital-loss carryovers, special capital gains rates, and RMD excise tax. It uses one non-Roth workplace plan per owner and the Uniform Lifetime Table. Inherited accounts, separate employer plans, and the joint-life table for a sole-beneficiary spouse more than 10 years younger need separate calculations. Qualified charitable distributions can count toward an eligible IRA RMD, but are outside the model. The 2027 limit is $114,000. [IRS Publication 590-B](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b) · [2027 retirement adjustments](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-66.pdf)

For the **1959 birth cohort**, the final regulation cited here reserves the applicable age and Treasury proposed age 73. That cohort is below either applicable age in 2027. Before releasing a tax year reaching that cohort's age 73, verify the latest final guidance. The current cohort rules flag the unresolved ages if carried into a later profile without an update. [Final regulation](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title26-vol6/xhtml/CFR-2025-title26-vol6-sec1-401a9-2.html) · [Proposed rule](https://www.irs.gov/irb/2024-33_IRB)

A conversion can affect Medicare income-related premiums under the usual two-year lookback. Medicare premiums and health-insurance subsidy changes remain outside the projection. [SSA IRMAA guidance](https://secure.ssa.gov/apps10/poms.nsf/lnx/0601101010)
